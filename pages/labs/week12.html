Week 12 Lab — Cross-Team Review, Then a Phase 3 Plan

{{ site.course_code }} · {{ site.course_semester }}

Goal

Today another team reviews your project and you review theirs. Then you turn what you heard into a plan for Phase 3. Everyone has real work to do, whether or not your prototype runs today:

  • Track A, it runs: reviewers use your prototype themselves, try to make its AI feature fail, and check one pull request
  • Track B, it doesn't run end to end yet: reviewers try to get it running from your README, review your pull requests and decision log, and check your plan to get it working

A Track B review is not a lower grade or a lesser review. It is often the more useful one: an outside pair finding exactly where your setup breaks is what you need before CP3.

Working style: In-person lab, teams · Total time: ~60 minutes

The standard for every finding is lecture slide 21: where, what you saw (evidence), and a specific suggestion. “Looks good” and “could be clearer” are not findings.

Before Class (everyone)

  • main in the best state you can get it today. Merge day does not need to be done before the lab; it only needs to be done before you submit CP2
  • One laptop with your prototype ready to run, and one teammate who can run it without help
  • Your repository link, and your README up to date
  • Close anything private on the demo laptop: .env files, API keys, chats. Reviewers will be at your keyboard

Setup (5 minutes)

  1. Find your pairing in the pairing table at the end of this guide. Find your group in the left column: the group beside it is the one you review. Find your group in the right column: the group beside it reviews you. These are two different groups.
  2. Put out your status card. Write one line and place it next to your laptop:
    • Track A: “Our main task runs end to end.” or
    • Track B: “It doesn't run end to end yet. It breaks at: ____.”
    Be honest. Saying where it breaks gets you a useful review. Pretending it works gets you a confused one.
  3. Split your team in two pairs: in Round 1, pair 1 hosts (stays with your laptop and answers questions) and pair 2 reviews (goes to the team you review). In Round 2 you swap.

Part 1 — Cross-Team Review (25 minutes: two rounds of about 12)

Hosts: answer questions, show where things are, and do not touch the keyboard unless the reviewers ask. Take notes of what surprised the reviewers; that is feedback too.

Reviewers: read the team's status card, then follow Track A or Track B. Write findings into the review form (week12_lab_submission_template.md, Part 1) as you go. Round 2 reviewers continue the same form: build on what Round 1 found, do not repeat it.

Resource for A2 and B4: the failure-states activity from today's lecture (what the user sees when the AI is wrong, slow or unavailable). If your team filled one in, hosts can show it; reviewers can compare it with what actually happens. It is not required.

Track A: The Prototype Runs

StepWhat to doMinutes
A1Use it yourselves. Ask the hosts for the main task in one sentence, then do it, with a reviewer at the keyboard. Where did you hesitate? What was unclear?4
A2Make the AI feature fail (lecture slide 24). Try empty input, odd input, very long input, something off-topic. What does the user see? Can they recover?3
A3Trust and transparency (slides 23, 25). Can a user tell the output came from AI? Can they correct or reject it easily?2
A4One pull request. Pick one recently merged PR. Could you understand what it did, what the AI did, and how it was checked, from its description alone? Use the reviewer checklist (slide 18) on its diff3

Track B: The Prototype Doesn't Run End to End Yet

StepWhat to doMinutes
B1Where does it break? Ask the hosts to show the furthest point it gets to. Then try the README yourselves: could a new person set it up? Note the first step that fails or is missing4
B2Two pull requests. Pick one merged and one open PR. Review them with the reviewer checklist (slide 18) and ask: from the description alone, what did the AI do and how was it checked?4
B3The plan. Read recent DECISIONS.md entries (and the merge plan, if they have written one yet). Is there a clear, small plan to get the main task running? Who owns it, and by when?2
B4The design on paper. Look at the design rationale: when the AI is wrong or unavailable, what is meant to happen?2

Partly running? Do A1 on the part that works and B1 on the part that doesn't, then continue with whichever of A4 or B2 fits.

What Every Review Must Contain

  • At least three findings, each with where (a screen, a file, a PR number), what you saw (evidence), and one specific suggestion
  • Severity for each: blocks the main task / will confuse users or reviewers / polish
  • One genuine strength, specific enough that they know what to keep doing

Part 2 — Turn the Review You Received Into a Plan (15 minutes, your own team)

  1. Read the review you received together (the other team sends it before the end of Part 1, or reads it out to you).
  2. Make GitHub issues for the findings you will act on: one issue per finding, with a label (for example cp3, bug, ux, process), an owner and a target date. Write “not doing, because…” for any finding you decide not to act on.
  3. Track B teams: make one extra issue, “Main task runs end to end”, with the smallest next step, an owner and a date this week.
  4. Add the PR template. Copy pull_request_template.md (in the lab files) to .github/pull_request_template.md in your repository, adjust it if you like, and open a pull request for it. That pull request itself should use the template. Get it reviewed and merged by your steward.

Part 3 — Guidelines from Evidence (15 minutes, your own team)

  1. Look at your evidence: the three numbers from lecture slide 4 (PRs mostly AI-generated, explainable by someone else, saying what the AI did), today's review, and your current AI usage guidelines.
  2. Rewrite three rules into rules a teammate could check from a pull request (module slide 5). For each one, write the old wording, the new wording, and the evidence that made you change it.
  3. Commit the updated guidelines to your repository (through a pull request, using your new template).
  4. Evaluation plan: in two sentences, what will you change in your evaluation plan for Phase 3, given what the reviewers saw or could not see?

Submission Checklist

  • Part 1: the review we wrote (at least three findings with where, evidence, suggestion and severity; one strength), and we sent it to the other team
  • Part 2: the review we received; links to our GitHub issues (with owners and dates); Track B: the “runs end to end” issue
  • Part 2: link to the merged PR that adds .github/pull_request_template.md
  • Part 3: three rules (old, new, evidence), the commit or PR link, and two sentences on the evaluation plan
  • Saved as one PDF named GroupXX_Week12_Lab.pdf and uploaded to E3 under “Week 12 Lab”

If Things Go Wrong

ProblemWhat to do
The other team's prototype stops working during your reviewSwitch to Track B from where you are; that is a finding
Your laptop or the network failsTell the reviewers, switch your status card to Track B, show the repository instead
Your repository is private and the reviewers can't open itHosts share the screen or open the pages for them; send the review by email instead of an issue
The team you review is missing membersReview with whoever is there; a single host is fine
The group you review is absent, or the group that reviews you isAsk a TA: you will be paired with another group, or a TA will review you
You run out of time in Part 3Finish the rules after class, but before the hand-in
