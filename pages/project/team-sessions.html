Walking Skeleton and Merge Day

Team Sessions Guide

Before CP2, your team holds two whole-team sessions: the walking skeleton and merge day. They are short, they happen outside class, and all four of you take part. Both must be done before you submit CP2 (Sunday, November 29th, end of day), and each leaves a small record that is part of your CP2 submission.

Walking skeletonMerge day
Whenfirst — do it as soon as your team has initial pieces to connectlast — right before you submit CP2, with enough time to fix what it uncovers
How longabout 75 minutesabout 60 minutes, plus 15 minutes of steward prep
Goalthe thinnest version of your tool runs from input to outputa clean main, running end to end
What you hand in (in CP2)the worksheet (docs/walking_skeleton.md) and a link to the merged PRthe merge plan entry in DECISIONS.md, with its outcome, and the worksheet (docs/merge_day.md)
Worksheetboth worksheets are in the team sessions materials on E3

Why these sessions exist. In a team project it is easy for four people to work in four separate corners and only discover at the end that the pieces do not fit. These sessions are the moments when everyone works on the same code, at the same time, and checks that it connects. How you work together in them matters more than how much you produce.

How they are graded. In the CP2 rubric, “Team sessions” is worth 10 points. We grade that the sessions happened, that all four of you took part, and how honestly you reflected, not how impressive the output was. A session where you found a problem and wrote it down is a good session.

Walking Skeleton

What It Is

The thinnest possible version of your tool that runs from beginning to end: a user gives something in, something comes out. It can be ugly. Parts can be faked. What matters is that the pieces connect, and that you find out early if they do not.

Before the Session

  • Pick a time all four of you can make. Same room, or one call where you can share a screen
  • Make sure everyone can clone and run the project on their own machine: each of you will build a piece
  • Open the worksheet in your repository so one person can type into it during the session
  • API keys stay in environment variables. Never paste a key into the worksheet, a PR or a chat

During the Session (about 75 minutes)

PartTimeWhat you do
1 ★10 minEach person writes one sentence about what the tool does, alone; read them aloud; agree on one
2 ★10 minDraw the path from input to output (three to five boxes). Not running yet: decide the smallest piece that counts as “it runs”. Already running: trace it (each box explained by someone who didn't write it) and pick the next thin slice. Then split the building into one task per person or pair, and agree what each piece receives and returns
3 ★30 minBuild, then connect. 20 minutes: each person or pair builds their piece on their own branch (stuck? fake it at your edge, in the agreed format). 10 minutes, together on one screen: connect the pieces one at a time; each piece is explained by someone who didn't write it before it goes in; run the example end to end
4 ★15 minOpen the pull request together. The current code steward reviews it on screen and leaves at least one real comment; the author answers; merge
55 minTeam check: what worked, what was awkward, who had the least chance to contribute, what you will do differently next time

★ = protect these if time runs short.

Already have something running? You still do the session. Part 2 gives you a route: trace the path together, then build the next thin slice (something still faked, the second user, or one failure state for the AI call).

After the Session

  • Save the worksheet as docs/walking_skeleton.md
  • Add one line to DECISIONS.md about the biggest choice you made (what you cut, or what you faked)
  • Keep the link to the merged pull request for CP2

Tips

  • Agreeing on the sentence is the most valuable ten minutes. Teams that skip it often discover in Week 14 that they were building different tools
  • Agree the hand-offs before you split. Most connection failures are two pieces that disagree about what they pass each other. Two minutes on “receives / returns” saves the connect step
  • Fake freely, but write it down. A faked response in the agreed format lets the others keep going, and is better than a half-built real one that blocks them
  • Every piece is explained by someone who didn't write it before it is connected. If nobody can explain AI-written code, make it shorter until someone can

Merge Day

What It Is

A session to clean up main before you submit CP2: every open pull request merged or closed, conflicts resolved together, and the prototype running end to end on a fresh copy. Nothing new goes in today.

Why Before CP2

CP2 asks for a working prototype, and the version you submit is the version we grade. Merge day makes sure that version is the one on main, that it runs, and that every member's work is in it, rather than spread across branches that never got merged. Plan it with enough time to fix what it uncovers before the deadline.

Before the Session

  • The current code steward (about 15 minutes) writes a merge plan as a new entry in DECISIONS.md: every open branch and PR, the order to merge them and why, the conflicts you expect, PRs you will close without merging, and what “done” means today. The worksheet has a template
  • Everyone else: push your work, and mark each of your PRs as ready, needing work, or to be closed. Read the steward's plan before the session starts

During the Session (about 60 minutes)

PartTimeWhat you do
15 minRead the plan together, adjust the order if needed, and decide who drives each merge (rotate)
2 ★35 minMerge one PR at a time, in order: update it with main, resolve conflicts together, run the tests and the app, review and merge, then run the tests and the app again on main before the next one
3 ★10 minFresh-clone test: someone who is not the steward clones the repository into a new folder, follows only the README, and does the main task end to end
4 ★5 minRecord the outcome under the merge plan in DECISIONS.md, and do a short team check

★ = protect these if time runs short.

After the Session

  • The merge plan entry, with its outcome, is your CP2 artifact
  • Save the worksheet as docs/merge_day.md
  • Anything left over becomes a GitHub issue for Phase 3

Tips

  • Conflicts are a team conversation. Read both sides out loud. If an AI tool suggests a resolution, the driver explains what it keeps and what it drops before you accept it
  • Never stack merges on a broken main. Stop, fix it with one small PR, or revert the last merge (git revert -m 1 <merge commit>) and write down why
  • The fresh-clone test is how we will see your project. If the README is not enough for a teammate, it will not be enough for whoever grades or reviews your CP2
  • Small, short-lived branches make merge day easy. If merging was painful, that is the habit to change in Phase 3

Questions

One of us cannot come. Do the session with the people who can, and write down who was missing and why. The missing member reads the worksheet afterwards and adds a line about it. If it keeps happening, tell a TA.

We are working remotely. That works: one call where you can share a screen. In the walking skeleton you build your pieces separately and share a screen to connect them; on merge day, the person typing changes for each merge.

We did not finish. Hand in what you have and write what happened. That counts. Not finishing because you discovered a real problem is the point of the session.

We already did a session without the worksheet. Fill in the worksheet from memory now, say so at the top, and link the PR or DECISIONS.md entry that came out of it.

Can we use AI tools during the sessions? Yes, as you would in normal work. The rule for both sessions: whoever is typing explains AI-written code or AI-suggested conflict resolutions before they are kept.

Who do we ask for help? A TA, or the teaching team by email. If something is going wrong in the team, tell us early.
