Week 2 Self-Paced Module: In-Context Learning and Instruction Tuning

{{ site.course_code }} · {{ site.course_semester }}

Overview

This module answers a question Week 2's lecture raised but left open: if the model is just predicting tokens, why does it follow instructions at all? And why do examples in the prompt work without retraining? Neither in-context learning nor instruction following is magic — both have specific mechanisms, and understanding those mechanisms changes how you design prompts and systems.

Why This Matters

Understanding why few-shot examples work tells you how to choose better examples. Understanding why instruction tuning is not a hard constraint tells you when and why your system prompt might fail — including to adversarial input from users. These are practical, not just theoretical, engineering concerns for the systems you'll build this semester.

What You'll Learn

  • In-context learning — giving a model examples doesn't change its weights; it shifts the probability distribution over what comes next. This is why example quality matters more than example quantity, and why output can be sensitive to example order and phrasing.
  • Why raw models don't follow instructions — a model trained to predict internet text learns to produce internet text, not to answer questions. Instruction following has to be introduced deliberately through fine-tuning.
  • Three approaches to instruction tuning — FLAN (fine-tuning on diverse tasks phrased as instructions), InstructGPT/RLHF (training on human preference rankings), and Constitutional AI (using the model's own self-critique against written principles instead of human rankers).
  • Engineering implications — how to write more effective few-shot examples and system prompts, why RLHF training produces sycophancy (covered further in Week 6), and why instruction following being learned rather than hardcoded creates prompt injection risk for any system that processes user-supplied text.

Optional Materials

If you want to go deeper on any of these topics (not required, but helpful if you're curious):

  • Chip Huyen, “RLHF: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback,” 2023 — explains why raw pre-trained models don't follow instructions, and discusses InstructGPT in real depth.
  • Anthropic, “Claude's Character,” 2024 — a readable explanation of how Constitutional AI shapes Claude's behaviour; connects instruction tuning to what you will observe in the API.
  • Jason Wei et al., “Finetuned Language Models Are Zero-Shot Learners” (FLAN), 2021 — the original FLAN paper (read just the abstract and introduction); shows what instruction tuning looked like in practice at the research level.
  • Riley Goodside, via Simon Willison, “prompt injection examples,” 2022 — short and practical; makes the security implication of instruction tuning immediately concrete.
