# Checkpoint 1: Rubric

CP1 is scored out of 100 points and counts for 5% of your course grade (team grade). The rubric rewards specific, honest, evidence-based work. It is not a measure of how polished the document looks.

| Section | Points |
|---|---|
| 1. Team charter | 15 |
| 2. Problem statement with evidence | 30 |
| 3. Initial interaction design sketch | 15 |
| 4. Draft evaluation plan | 30 |
| 5. Repository | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## 1. Team charter (15 points)

| Criterion | Pts | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Decisions when there is disagreement** | 5 | A concrete process another team member could follow (who decides, what happens if the team cannot agree). | Stated but vague, such as "we discuss it" (2–3). | Missing (0). |
| **Definition of done** | 5 | Observable criteria that someone could check for a piece of work (for example reviewed, merged, DECISIONS.md updated). | Generic, such as "it works" (2–3). | Missing (0). |
| **Communication outside class** | 5 | Channel, how often, and what response time you expect from each other. | One of those three is missing (3). | Missing (0). |

## 2. Problem statement with evidence (30 points)

| Criterion | Pts | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | 10 | Names the exact problem and who experiences it, concrete enough that two readers picture the same situation. | Right area but still broad (5–7). | Generic, such as "teams have trouble communicating" (0–2). |
| **Evidence from real users** | 12 | At least two people outside your team who actually experience the problem; for each, who they are and what they told you, with specifics (not just "they agreed"). | Two conversations but the summaries are generic or show only agreement (7–9). Only one conversation (6). | No conversations, or only teammates (0–3). |
| **Why existing tools do not solve it** | 8 | Names at least one specific existing tool or approach and says what it fails to do for this problem, tied to what users told you. | Generic, such as "existing tools are not good enough" (3–5). | Missing (0–2). |

## 3. Initial interaction design sketch (15 points)

| Criterion | Pts | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What the tool does** | 5 | A clear description (text or diagram) that matches the problem you described. | Clear but not connected to the problem, or hard to follow (2–3). | Missing (0). |
| **Inputs and outputs** | 5 | Says what goes in and what comes out, specifically enough to build from. | Only one of the two, or too vague to build from (2–3). | Missing (0). |
| **How two people interact with it** | 5 | Shows what each person does and how work passes between them, not just "both can use it". | Mentions two people but not how they interact (2–3). | Missing (0). |

## 4. Draft evaluation plan (30 points)

| Criterion | Pts | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Success definition** | 8 | "We will know our tool works if..." is observable, measurable and about what happens for users, not only what the tool produces. | Observable but measures only output (such as "it generates summaries"), or not measurable (4–5). | Missing or not checkable (0–2). |
| **Target users and access** | 5 | Who you will test with (outside your team) and how you will reach them. | Who, but not how you will reach them (3). | Missing (0). |
| **Method and timing** | 5 | One approach named (structured observation, pre/post survey or brief interview) and roughly when it fits in the semester. | Method without timing, or timing that does not fit the semester (3). | Missing (0). |
| **Minimum evidence threshold** | 6 | States concretely what result would convince a skeptic (for example how many users, what change). | Stated but a skeptic would not be convinced (3–4). | Missing (0–1). |
| **Human-AI boundary** | 6 | If your tool uses AI: what the AI decides and what the human decides. If it does not: says so and why. Either earns full credit. | One side only, or an honest "not decided yet" that says how and when you will decide (3–4). | Missing (0–1). |

## Notes for students

- Saying clearly that you are unsure about something (your users, your method, whether the idea will work) is never penalised. Claims that go beyond your evidence are.
- Your design may change later in the semester. Changing it is not penalised.
- Two conversations is the minimum for full marks on the evidence criterion. More conversations are valuable but do not earn extra points here.
- File format and naming are not graded unless they stop the TAs from reading your submission.
